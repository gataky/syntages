---
title: Chinese Eggplant
time: 30 min
---

## Method

1. Cut the eggplant into bite-size pieces, then prepare it one of two ways:

    1. **Soak:** cover with water, mix in the salt and keep the eggplant submerged under a pot lid for 15 minutes. Drain and pat dry.
    2. **Salt:** spread the pieces on paper towel, sprinkle both sides with kosher salt and leave for 45–60 minutes. Pat dry without rinsing.

    - 300 g Chinese long eggplant (2 small)
    - 1 tsp salt
2. Mix the sauce in a small bowl.
    - 1 tbsp light soy sauce (or regular soy sauce)
    - 1 tbsp water
    - ½ tsp dark soy sauce, optional
    - 2 tsp sugar
    - 1 tsp cornstarch, for the sauce
3. Toss the eggplant with the cornstarch by hand until it's evenly coated in a thin layer.
    - 1 tbsp cornstarch
4. Heat the oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium-high heat until hot. Spread the eggplant across the pan without overlapping and cook one side at a time until charred all over and soft, 8–10 minutes in total. Turn the heat down to medium if the pan starts to smoke. Transfer to a plate.
    - 2 tbsp peanut oil (or vegetable oil)
5. Add the remaining oil, the ginger and garlic to the skillet and stir until fragrant. Return the eggplant. Stir the sauce again to dissolve the cornstarch, pour it over and stir straight away until the eggplant is coated and the sauce thickens.
    - ½ tbsp peanut oil (or vegetable oil)
    - 1 tsp minced ginger
    - 3 cloves garlic, chopped
6. Serve hot, as a side or as a main over steamed rice or noodles.

## Notes

- Other eggplants will crisp up this way too, but Asian long eggplant works best.
- There's very little sauce, just enough to coat the eggplant. It's rich, which helps the eggplant stay crisp. It reduces very fast once it hits the pan, so stir immediately.
- The dark soy sauce is mostly for colour. Skip it if you don't have any.
